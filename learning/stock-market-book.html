FOREWORD

How to read this book

This book is built around one premise: you learn markets faster when every concept is tied to an action you can take the same evening. Every chapter pairs theory with a hands-on item — read a CSV, compute a ratio, paper trade a setup — and ends with a small checklist so you know whether you can move on.

It is India-specific. Numbers, file names, regulators, and timings refer to NSE and BSE. Most of the same ideas hold globally, but the tape, the data sources, and the regulatory mechanics differ — so this stays focused.

The chapters are sequenced as Phase 0 → Phase 6. You can skip ahead, but each phase assumes the one before it. Phase 3, which covers participant data after market close, is the chapter most readers will return to repeatedly — bookmark it.

Two recurring boxes:

  • EXERCISE a small, concrete task — usually 10–30 minutes.
  • DONE CHECK the questions you should be able to answer before moving on.
Read with NSE's website open in another tab. Most of the value is in actually pulling the reports the book references and looking at the columns yourself.
PHASE 0

Foundations

Before any chart, any indicator, any strategy — there is the plumbing. What is a share, what is an exchange, who oversees them, and how does an order placed from your phone reach a matching engine, get matched, and become a holding in your demat account.

0.1 What a share actually is

A share is a fractional ownership claim on a company. Owning one share of Reliance Industries entitles you to a proportional slice of dividends declared, voting rights (one vote per share for ordinary shares), and a claim on residual assets in liquidation. The price of that share on any given day is whatever the marginal buyer and marginal seller agree on — not the company's "true" value, and not its book value.

Two layers of market exist for that share:

  • Primary market — where shares are created. IPO (Initial Public Offering), FPO (Follow-on), rights issue, QIP. Money flows from investor to company.
  • Secondary market — where existing shares change hands between investors. NSE, BSE. Money flows from buyer to seller; the company is uninvolved.

0.3 Indices

An index is a number that summarises a basket of stocks. The ones that matter for your bot:

  • NIFTY 50 — 50 large-cap NSE stocks, free-float market-cap weighted. The most-traded index on the planet by contract volume.
  • BANK NIFTY — 12 banking stocks. Very volatile; popular for intraday options.
  • FINNIFTY — broader financial services (banks + NBFCs + insurance).
  • MIDCPNIFTY — mid-cap.
  • SENSEX — BSE's 30-stock flagship. Similar role to NIFTY 50 but on BSE.

0.4 Order types you will use today

  • Market order — fills at best available price. Fast, but slippage-prone in illiquid contracts.
  • Limit order — fills only at your price or better. Default choice for anything except urgent exits.
  • Stop-loss — sits dormant until a trigger price is hit, then becomes a market or limit order (SL-M / SL).
  • GTT (Good-Till-Triggered) — broker-side trigger that survives across sessions (up to one year).

0.5 Settlement: T+1

India moved to T+1 settlement for all equities by January 2023, and is rolling out T+0 (same-day) and instant settlement in beta. For F&O, daily MTM (mark-to-market) is settled in cash; final settlement at expiry is cash for index derivatives and physical delivery for stock derivatives.

WHY PHYSICAL DELIVERY MATTERS
If you hold an in-the-money Reliance call on expiry day and don't square off, you must take delivery of the underlying shares — full contract value, not just the option premium. Many retail traders have been wiped out by forgetting this. Index options (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY) are cash-settled, so the bot is safe.

Your marketClock.js gates the signal engine to 09:45–14:00 IST. That's deliberately inside the volatile open (which is 09:15–09:45) and before the pre-close drift (after 14:00). The hour before close is technically tradable but is dominated by squaring-off flow, not directional flow.

1.3 Order types you will encounter

  • Market — immediate fill at best price. Avoid in illiquid options where the bid-ask can be 5%+.
  • Limit — fill at your price or better. Default.
  • SL (Stop-loss limit) — at trigger, becomes a limit order.
  • SL-M (Stop-loss market) — at trigger, becomes a market order. Banned on NIFTY and BANKNIFTY options since 2021 because of repeated flash spikes that wiped out retail.
  • GTT — broker-side, not exchange-side; survives sessions.
  • BO / CO — discontinued for retail by SEBI in 2020. Do not design around them.
  • AMO — after-market order; queued for next session's pre-open.

1.4 Circuit limits

Index circuits are 10% / 15% / 20%. Trading halts for 45 minutes / 1 hour 45 minutes / rest of day, depending on when in the session the band is breached. Triggered four times in NSE's history — Mar 2020 (COVID) twice and 2008 twice.

Stock circuits are per-stock bands of 2% / 5% / 10% / 20%. Stocks in F&O are exempt from daily bands but have a 10% "operating range" that can be widened intraday by the exchange.

PHASE 2

Market Participants — FII · DII · Pro · Client

NSE doesn't think of "the market" as a homogeneous crowd. For every F&O instrument it tracks four distinct buckets of traders. Knowing which bucket is on which side of a trade — and how that bucket usually behaves — is the single highest-leverage piece of information available to a retail trader for free.

2.1 The four buckets

CodeFull nameWho they areBehaviour stereotype
ClientRetail + HNIIndividuals, HUFs, small partnership firmsNet option buyers. Often herding. Frequently a contra-indicator.
DIIDomestic Institutional InvestorsIndian mutual funds, insurance (LIC the largest), banks' treasury, pension funds (EPFO, NPS)Slow, steady, counter-cyclical. Provide liquidity when FIIs sell.
FII / FPIForeign Portfolio InvestorsForeign hedge funds, global mutual funds, sovereign wealth funds, ETF flowsTrend-setters in cash; sophisticated hedgers in derivatives.
ProProprietary trading desksBrokers trading their own balance sheet — Zerodha Prop, Kotak Prop, Edelweiss Prop, etc.Short-dated, tactical, well-informed. Often lead the tape by 1–2 sessions.

2.2 What each bucket cares about

Client (retail)

Mostly buy options because the headline payoff is "lottery-like" — small premium, theoretically unlimited upside. The aggregate effect is that retail is structurally short gamma for the market makers on the other side. Pattern: heavy CE buying on green days, heavy PE buying on red days; both decay through theta before they pay off.

DII

Two flavours. Mutual funds are constrained by SIP inflows — when retail SIPs total ₹15,000 cr/month in 2026, MFs must deploy. That creates a persistent bid in large-cap cash, especially on red days. Insurance/LIC trades much larger blocks, often opportunistic, and increasingly active in index futures for portfolio hedging.

FII

Single biggest swing factor in Indian equities. When the dollar index (DXY) rises or US 10Y yields jump, FIIs typically sell EMs including India — even if Indian fundamentals are fine. The flow is part-mechanical (ETF rebalances) and part-discretionary (active funds). FII cash selling drags spot; FII F&O positioning often hedges or anticipates that.

Pro

The desk traders inside brokers. Tiny capital relative to FIIs but extremely active and information-rich. Their positioning in index futures and options is the closest free signal to "what the smart short-term money is doing." Watch for divergences: when Pros build longs while Clients build shorts in index futures, the next session is statistically more often green.

2.3 The data fields you'll actually see

NSE's daily Participant-wise OI file contains, for each of the four codes (Client / DII / FII / Pro):

  • Future Index Long
  • Future Index Short
  • Future Stock Long
  • Future Stock Short
  • Option Index Call Long
  • Option Index Call Short
  • Option Index Put Long
  • Option Index Put Short
  • Option Stock Long / Short
  • Total Long Contracts / Total Short Contracts

From those raw fields you compute:

  • Net position per bucket per instrument = Long − Short
  • Long/Short ratio per bucket — useful for index futures specifically
  • Day-over-day delta on the net — this is the action, not the snapshot

2.4 The classic divergence setups

ConfigurationTypical next-day bias
FII net-long futures & rising; Pros net-long & rising; Clients net-shortBullish — smart money positioned for up move
FII net-short futures while heavy PE writing; Pros also net-shortBearish but with limited downside (PE writers expect support)
FII short futures + heavy CE buyingHedge configuration — they expect volatility, direction unclear
Pros flipping from long to short over 2 sessions while Clients add longsBearish — Pros usually lead, retail usually lags
DII heavy cash buying while FII sells > ₹3,000 crSlow-bleed market — short-term weakness, long-term floor
REMEMBER THE T+1 DELAY
Participant-wise OI for today publishes tomorrow evening (~18:00 IST). NSE delays it deliberately to prevent front-running. So when you read FII positioning Tuesday night, it's Monday's snapshot — you act on it Wednesday morning. Plan your routine around this.

Direct link pattern: nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/nsccl/fao_participant_oi_{DDMMYYYY}.csv

It's a small file (~5 KB). Pull it once a day; archive it locally next to your bot's snapshot data and you can backtest participant-driven signals years in.

3.4 Participant-wise Volume

Same page on NSE. Same buckets. Tells you activity rather than position. Useful for distinguishing position-building from churn.

3.5 Bulk & Block deals

Two file types, distinct definitions:

  • Bulk deal — > 0.5% of company's listed shares in a single day (cumulative). Reported by broker by next business day.
  • Block deal — single trade ≥ ₹10 crore in the block deal window (08:45–09:00 or 14:05–14:20). Both parties matched at a single price.

These name the actual buyer/seller — "Morgan Stanley Mauritius Bought 2.1 cr shares of XYZ at ₹178.40." Track over weeks and you learn which FII/MF is accumulating which stock.

3.6 SAST & insider disclosures

Required filings under SEBI's takeover code (SAST) and insider trading regulations. When promoters / directors / large shareholders buy or sell their own company's shares above thresholds, they must disclose within two trading days. Available on NSE/BSE corporate filings pages and aggregated by Trendlyne.

3.8 The evening routine (45 minutes, four nights a week)

  1. 18:00 — open NSE Participant OI for the latest available date. Note net FII / Pro positions in index futures and the change vs previous day.
  2. 18:05 — refresh FII / DII cash provisional. Note net and 5-day cumulative.
  3. 18:10 — open today's F&O bhavcopy. Identify max-OI call strike and max-OI put strike for the nearest weekly expiry. These are likely resistance and support.
  4. 18:20 — compute PCR(OI) for nearest expiry. Note it next to a rolling chart of the last 30 days.
  5. 18:30 — write three lines in your journal. What changed today. What the data is biased towards. What you'll watch for at the open tomorrow.

PHASE 4

Reading the data — the interpretation playbook

Raw data tells you what happened. Interpretation tells you what to do tomorrow. This chapter is a playbook of recurring patterns and how to act on them.

4.1 The seven-question morning checklist

  1. What did FIIs do in cash yesterday? Direction, magnitude vs 20-day mean, streak length.
  2. What did DIIs do in cash yesterday? Same. Importantly — were they absorbing FII selling or were both on the same side?
  3. What's FII net position in NIFTY futures (latest available)? Long, short, or neutral? Direction of change vs previous day?
  4. What did Pros do in NIFTY futures? Are they on the same side as FIIs or opposite? Pros vs FIIs divergence is information.
  5. Where is the heaviest CE OI sitting? That strike is likely resistance.
  6. Where is the heaviest PE OI sitting? Likely support.
  7. What's the PCR(OI) today vs last week? Rising PCR = bullish skew building. Falling = bearish.

4.2 Patterns that recur

Pattern A — The classic short squeeze setup

FII net-short index futures sitting at multi-month high. Pros also net-short. CE OI building at strikes just above spot (writers expecting a cap). Then a positive catalyst — global cues, RBI surprise, earnings beat. Shorts unwind violently. NIFTY can gap up 1.5% and trend the whole session.

Trade idea: when FII net-short futures > 75th percentile of last 90 days and CE call writers heavy at near-the-money, an ATM CE on a positive global open is high-asymmetry.

Pattern B — The slow bleed

FII selling cash for 4+ consecutive days; DII absorbing but with reducing daily amount. PCR(OI) falling steadily, indicating PE writers losing conviction. Pros adding shorts. This is not crash mode — it's a managed decline. Index moves down 0.3–0.7% per session.

Trade idea: ATM PE with tight stop, or a bear call spread to harvest theta on writers.

Pattern C — The pre-event coil

Major event ahead (RBI MPC, US FOMC, election result, budget). Option IV spikes. Both CE and PE OI build at far OTM strikes (clients buying lottery). Pros write the wings. Spot pinned to max-pain strike. Very narrow daily ranges.

Trade idea: short strangle if you have margin discipline; if not, stay flat — directional trades have terrible risk-reward in this regime.

Pattern D — The trend day

Pre-open shows large gap (> 0.6%). FII net buyers in pre-market via NIFTY futures on SGX/GIFT. First 15 minutes after open show heavy volume and no retracement. Bank Nifty leading.

Trade idea: trend-follow ATM in direction of gap; avoid reversals.

4.3 The three traps

  • Single-day flow over-reading — one day of ₹500 cr FII selling means little. Look at 5-day cumulative.
  • Participant OI lag confusion — Monday's number publishes Tuesday evening; you trade on it Wednesday. Don't act as if it's real-time.
  • PCR worship — PCR is context, not a trigger. Markets can stay overbought (PCR > 1.5) for weeks.

4.4 Connecting this to your bot

Your current signal engine in signal/node-engine/signalEngine.js scores intraday OI changes per strike to identify BULL or BEAR setups. The next natural extension is to ingest the participant-wise OI file each evening and add a feature: FII-Pro alignment in index futures. When FIIs and Pros are on the same side and both adding, the bot can upweight a same-direction intraday signal. When they're opposite, downweight. We can wire that as a small JS module that reads the CSV from data/fno/participant-oi/ and outputs a {-1, 0, +1} bias each morning.

PHASE 5

Derivatives Deep Dive

You can read the tape now. This chapter is where you learn the instruments themselves — futures and options — in enough depth to design strategies rather than borrow them.

5.1 Futures

A futures contract is an obligation to buy or sell an underlying at a fixed price on a fixed date. In India, equity index futures (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY) are cash-settled at expiry. Each contract has a defined lot size — currently 75 for NIFTY, 30 for BANKNIFTY, 40 for FINNIFTY (subject to NSE revisions).

Cost of carry, contango, backwardation

Futures price ≠ spot price. The relationship: F = S × e^((r − d) × t) where r is risk-free rate, d is expected dividend yield, t is time to expiry. In normal conditions futures trade above spot (contango). When demand for shorts spikes (panic, hedging frenzy), futures can trade below spot (backwardation) — a useful sentiment signal.

Rollover

On the last Thursday of each month, near-month futures expire. Traders roll positions to the next month. Rollover percentage = open interest rolled to next month / total OI in expiring month. Healthy bull rollover > 75% with rising next-month basis indicates conviction. Weak rollover with collapsing basis indicates topping.

5.2 Options — first principles

An option is a right, not an obligation. The buyer pays a premium for the right; the seller (writer) collects the premium and accepts the obligation. Two kinds:

  • Call (CE) — right to buy at strike. Profitable when underlying rises.
  • Put (PE) — right to sell at strike. Profitable when underlying falls.

Option price = intrinsic value + time value. Intrinsic = max(S−K, 0) for calls, max(K−S, 0) for puts. Time value = whatever's left, and it decays to zero by expiry.

5.3 The Greeks

GreekWhat it measuresPractical takeaway
Delta (Δ)Change in option price per ₹1 move in underlyingATM ≈ 0.5. Deep ITM ≈ 1. Far OTM ≈ 0.
Gamma (Γ)Change in delta per ₹1 movePeaks at ATM. Highest near expiry. Sellers fear gamma.
Theta (Θ)Daily decay of option priceBuyers' enemy, sellers' friend. Accelerates near expiry.
Vega (ν)Change per 1% change in IVLong options = long vol. Volatility spike helps buyers.
Rho (ρ)Change per 1% change in interest ratesMostly negligible for short-dated options.

5.4 Implied volatility

IV is the market's forward-looking estimate of volatility, backed out of the option price using Black-Scholes. Two derived measures matter:

  • IV Percentile — what % of the last 12 months had a lower IV than today's? IVP > 70 means options are historically expensive; favours selling. IVP < 30 means cheap; favours buying.
  • IV Rank — similar but linear: (current IV − 52w low) / (52w high − 52w low).

5.6 Expiry-day mechanics

Each weekly expiry day (NIFTY: Thursday by current SEBI rules) has its own physics:

  • Theta is highest — OTM options bleed through the morning.
  • Pin risk — index gravitates toward max-pain strike as writers defend it.
  • Gamma spikes for ATM — small moves cause large premium swings.
  • The 0DTE trade — buying OTM 1-2 hours before close hoping for a tail move. Lottery; mostly loses; occasionally 10x. Plan size accordingly.

PHASE 6

Smart Money Concepts (SMC)

SMC is a price-action framework built on one premise: the market moves to take liquidity, not to be fair. Institutions — FIIs, banks, prop desks — need to fill orders far too large to be absorbed at a single price. They engineer moves that bait retail into the wrong side, then trade against the very flow they baited. SMC is the toolkit for reading those footprints on a chart.

This chapter is taught zero to advanced. By the end you will be able to mark up a NIFTY chart with order blocks, liquidity pools, BOS, CHoCH, and entries — and combine that with the participant-OI work from Phase 2–3 for a complete picture of who is doing it and where.

6.1 The premise — why SMC works

  • Retail places stops at obvious places: just below the previous swing low, just above the previous swing high, under round numbers, below the prior day's low.
  • These cluster into liquidity pools — pockets of resting orders that smart money can fill against.
  • Big orders cannot fill silently. To buy a thousand contracts, an institution needs a thousand sellers — and the cheapest way to get sellers is to scare retail into selling. (And vice versa for distribution.)
  • So the market is engineered through three repeating phases — accumulation, manipulation, distribution — that show up fractally on every timeframe.

6.2 PO3 — the Power of Three (the daily script)

Every quality trading session repeats this three-act play:

PhaseWhat price doesWhat retail thinksWhat smart money is doing
1. AccumulationSideways range, low volatility"Boring, nothing happening"Quietly loading positions inside the range
2. ManipulationSharp fake breakout in the opposite direction of the real move"Breakout! Chase!"Running retail stops, filling remaining orders
3. DistributionReal trend in the original (smart-money) direction"Wait, why is it going the other way?"Selling into retail buying (or buying retail selling)

Mapped to a typical NIFTY intraday session:

  • 09:15 – 09:30 — first 15-minute range. This is often the day's accumulation zone.
  • 09:30 – 10:00 — manipulation window. Price often pokes above or below the opening range to grab stops.
  • 10:00 – 14:00 — distribution / real trend. Most clean trends print here.
  • 14:00 – 15:30 — pre-close drift, hedging flow, often counter-trend retracements.

6.3 Market structure — BOS and CHoCH

Market structure is the sequence of swing highs and swing lows that defines the trend.

Uptrend

Higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL). Pattern: HL → HH → HL → HH …

Downtrend

Lower lows (LL) and lower highs (LH). Pattern: LH → LL → LH → LL …

BOS (Break of Structure)

Continuation. In an uptrend, when price breaks the last HH, that's a bullish BOS — the trend continues. In a downtrend, breaking the last LL is a bearish BOS.

CHoCH (Change of Character)

First sign of reversal. In an uptrend, when price breaks the most recent HL (not the HH), that's a bullish-to-bearish CHoCH. The character of the market has changed — buyers can no longer defend the last pullback. CHoCH is the earliest reliable reversal signal in SMC.

RULE OF THUMB
BOS = continuation. CHoCH = reversal alarm. After a CHoCH, you stop looking for buys (in a topping market) and start hunting sells — but only after a pullback into a fresh bearish order block.

6.4 Liquidity — where stops live

Liquidity is the fuel. Mark these levels on every chart:

  • BSL (buy-side liquidity) — resting orders above the market. Sit above swing highs, above equal highs, above PDH (previous day high), above weekly high. When price wicks through, smart money is taking BSL.
  • SSL (sell-side liquidity) — resting orders below the market. Below swing lows, equal lows, PDL, weekly low.
  • Equal highs / equal lows — magnets. Retail draws horizontal resistance/support; smart money sees a pool to drain.
  • Trendline liquidity — when retail draws a trendline, stops cluster on the wrong side of it. Smart money breaks the trendline to grab them.
  • PDH / PDL / IBH / IBL — previous day high/low, initial balance (first hour) high/low.
  • GIFT NIFTY overnight high / low — the Indian "Asian range." Often swept at the cash open.

6.5 Order Blocks (OB)

An order block is the last opposite-direction candle before an impulsive move. Mechanically: it's where institutions placed the orders that caused the impulse, so when price comes back to that zone they often defend it.

  • Bullish OB — the last bearish (red) candle before a strong up-move. Mark its body or full range. Look for buys when price returns to it.
  • Bearish OB — the last bullish (green) candle before a strong down-move. Look for sells when price returns to it.
  • Mitigation — the act of price returning to test the OB. Most plays happen on the first mitigation; subsequent mitigations are weaker.
  • Breaker block — an OB that failed (price broke through it). The role flips: a failed bullish OB becomes a bearish breaker on retest.

6.6 Fair Value Gaps (FVG / Imbalance)

A 3-candle pattern. Candle 1 high and candle 3 low (in a bullish move) don't overlap — the middle candle leaves a gap of unfilled price. The market treats this as unfinished business and tends to come back to fill it.

  • FVGs near recent structure are more reliable than FVGs deep in a trend.
  • An FVG that overlaps an OB is a high-confluence zone — many SMC traders only take trades at OB + FVG overlap.

6.7 Premium & Discount

For any impulse leg you're working with, find the 50% midpoint.

  • Price above 50% = premium → favours selling.
  • Price below 50% = discount → favours buying.
  • The 50% line itself is "equilibrium" — institutions often defend it.

Combined with structure: in an uptrend, buy only at discount of the latest bullish leg. Don't chase price at premium.

6.8 Inducement — the trap before the trap

Inducement is a small fake structure designed to bait retail before the real liquidity sweep. Example:

  1. NIFTY has a clean uptrend, with an obvious HL at 22,400.
  2. Smart money prints a small lower-low at 22,395 — looks like a bearish break, retail shorts.
  3. Price snaps back up, takes out the original HL stops at 22,400 (those shorts now stop out), and continues to the next BSL at 22,550.

The 22,395 dip was inducement. The 22,400 sweep was the actual liquidity grab. SMC traders learn to wait for the second move — the sweep of the obvious level — not the first.

6.9 SMT divergence

Two correlated instruments should move together. When one makes a new high or new low and its sibling doesn't, the new extreme is suspect. Examples in Indian markets:

  • NIFTY vs BANKNIFTY — NIFTY prints HH but BANKNIFTY only prints LH → bearish SMT, NIFTY top is likely false.
  • NIFTY vs GIFT NIFTY — intraday divergence between cash NIFTY and the futures listed on NSE IFSC.
  • NIFTY vs DOW / S&P — overnight session: if S&P closes at new highs but GIFT NIFTY doesn't follow, expect Indian weakness at open.

6.10 The "opposite people dump" mechanic — explicitly

This is the single most useful SMC idea once you've already built the participant-OI habit from Phase 3.

Smart money cannot fill a large position at a single price — they need a counterparty for every contract. So:

  • When they want to distribute (sell from inventory), they first induce retail to buy: engineer a breakout above an obvious resistance to trigger retail CE buying and breakout chasing. They sell into that demand. Once the supply is offloaded, price reverses and the retail CEs become worthless.
  • When they want to accumulate (buy into inventory), they first induce retail to sell: engineer a fake break below support to trigger panic and retail PE buying. They buy from the panic sellers. Then price reverses up.

Mathematically: retail aggregate position is roughly the inverse of smart money's intended fill. When you see Client OI heavily net-long index futures while FIIs and Pros are net-short — that's a distribution-loaded setup. The dump is being prepared.

CONNECTING SMC TO YOUR BOT
Your bot already computes intraday OI buildup at each strike. Add this layer: when Client = net long & FII + Pro = net short on the most recent participant-OI file, treat the day as a distribution-bias day. The bot should be quicker to short CE rallies and slower to chase CE breakouts.

6.11 Worked examples (NIFTY)

Example A — Bullish reversal off PDL sweep

  1. Previous day's low (PDL) = 22,380. Mark it on the chart.
  2. Today's session opens 22,420 and drifts down through morning.
  3. At 10:45 NIFTY spikes to 22,365 — wicks below PDL by 15 points, then closes the 5-min candle back at 22,395. SSL grabbed.
  4. The very next 5-min candle prints a strong bullish engulfing closing at 22,440 — that's a CHoCH on 5-min (previous lower-high at 22,430 broken).
  5. Find the order block: the last bullish candle that pushed up to 22,430 — say it was the 09:55 candle, range 22,408–22,425. That's your bullish OB.
  6. Enter long when price retraces to 22,420 (inside the OB). Stop just below the sweep low (22,360). Target the day's BSL at 22,510 (previous day high cluster).
  7. Reward/risk ≈ 90 / 60 = 1.5×, scaled up by the high-probability setup.

Example B — Bearish dump after BSL sweep at equal highs

  1. NIFTY ranges between 22,500 and 22,560 for two days. Equal highs at 22,560 are obvious.
  2. Participant OI from previous evening: Client +1.8 lakh long contracts on index futures; FII −1.5 lakh net. Distribution setup loaded.
  3. At 11:20 NIFTY breaks 22,560, prints 22,578, and pulls back to 22,555 within 10 minutes. BSL grabbed; CHoCH on 5-min.
  4. The OB is the last bearish 5-min candle before the spike — say 22,553–22,565.
  5. Short on retest of OB around 22,560. Stop above 22,585. Target previous range low at 22,500, then PDL at 22,460.
  6. Retail CE buyers who chased the 22,560 breakout are now underwater; their stops at 22,545 add fuel to the dump.

Example C — Inducement trap on a clean trendline

  1. BANKNIFTY in clean uptrend, four HLs forming a visible trendline.
  2. Price breaks the trendline at 48,200 — retail shorts on the "breakdown." This is inducement.
  3. Price wicks down to 48,150, sweeping SSL below the previous HL at 48,170. Then snaps up to 48,300, stopping all the breakout shorts.
  4. BOS bullish above 48,300 confirms continuation; trend resumes to next BSL at 48,600.
  5. Lesson: trendline breaks are the most common retail trap. Wait for the sweep and CHoCH, not the break.

Example D — SMT divergence reversal

  1. Late session 14:30. NIFTY pushes to a new intraday high at 22,490. BANKNIFTY at the same minute fails to break its own intraday high — prints a lower high.
  2. That's a bearish SMT — NIFTY's high is unconfirmed.
  3. Within 15 minutes NIFTY rejects, prints a CHoCH on 5-min, and closes the session at 22,420.
  4. The setup is especially clean near the close because intraday participants are squaring off — gives less follow-through risk.

6.12 Timeframe hierarchy

SMC works on every timeframe but only the right way: bias from higher timeframe (HTF), entry on lower timeframe (LTF).

TimeframeWhat you read from it
WeeklyMajor liquidity (multi-week swing H/L), macro structure
DailyBias for the day, PDH/PDL, daily OBs
1-hourIntraday structure, HTF order blocks worth watching
15-minConfirm CHoCH/BOS, locate refined OB
5-min / 1-minExecution: entry candle, stop placement

6.13 Common mistakes

  • Marking every wick as a "sweep." A real sweep is a wick that clearly takes out an obvious level and immediately reverses with momentum — not every minor poke.
  • Too many order blocks. Only the most recent untested OB at the relevant structural level matters. Erase the rest.
  • Ignoring HTF. A perfect 5-min bullish setup that sits inside a daily bearish OB will fail. Always check HTF first.
  • Trading without confluence. One signal alone (just a CHoCH, just an OB) is weak. Three confluences (CHoCH + OB + discount + SMT) is a high-probability trade.
  • Using SMC without participant data. SMC tells you where; OI / FII-Pro positioning tells you whether. Use both.

6.14 A clean SMC checklist for NIFTY intraday

  1. Before 09:15 — mark PDH, PDL, weekly H/L, GIFT NIFTY overnight H/L on chart.
  2. Check participant OI from previous evening — note bias (Client vs FII+Pro).
  3. Watch first 15 minutes; mark the opening range high/low.
  4. Wait for the first liquidity sweep of an obvious level (PDH/PDL/OR-high/OR-low).
  5. Confirm CHoCH on 5-min in the opposite direction of the sweep.
  6. Identify the OB or FVG that caused the CHoCH leg.
  7. Enter on retest of the OB; stop beyond the sweep wick.
  8. Target the opposite side's liquidity pool.
  9. Move stop to entry after 1R; trail or scale out at major levels.

Exercise — Phase 6

  1. Open last week's NIFTY 5-min chart for any one day. Mark PDH, PDL, opening range, swing highs & lows.
  2. Identify every liquidity sweep (wick through a level followed by close back inside) in that session.
  3. For each sweep, did a CHoCH follow on 5-min within the next 6 candles? If yes, mark the order block and the would-be trade.
  4. Note R-multiple of each setup using a stop just beyond the sweep wick.
  5. Cross-reference: was the participant-OI bias from the previous evening on the same side as your winning setups?

PHASE 7

Advanced & Quantitative

Once derivatives are second nature, the next layer is microstructure, volatility surfaces, macro overlays, and quantitative process. This chapter is shorter on prescription and longer on signposts — each topic is its own multi-month rabbit hole.

6.1 Market microstructure

  • Order book dynamics — limit orders form depth; market orders consume it. Bid-ask spread is the cost of immediacy.
  • Market makers — quote both sides; profit on spread; hedge inventory with futures.
  • HFT & latency arbitrage — sub-millisecond strategies; not your game, but understand they exist.
  • Iceberg orders — large orders shown in small visible slices.

6.2 Volatility surface

For a given expiry, plot IV across strikes — you don't get a flat line; you get a smile or skew. Indian index options typically show a put skew (OTM puts more expensive than equivalent OTM calls) because the market pays up for crash protection. The shape of the skew is itself tradeable.

Then layer expiry on the X-axis — that's the term structure. Normal: longer-dated options have higher IV. Inverted (front-month higher): event imminent, expected volatility front-loaded.

6.4 Quantitative process

Your bot is already in this territory. The disciplines you'll grow into:

  • Backtesting — walk-forward, never look-ahead bias, account for slippage and costs. Your paperTrader.js + diagnostics CSV are exactly this in live form.
  • Position sizing — fixed fractional, Kelly criterion (½-Kelly is a sane practical version), volatility-adjusted.
  • Risk metrics — Sharpe (return/total vol), Sortino (return/downside vol), max drawdown, Calmar (return/max DD).
  • Regime detection — trend vs mean-reversion; high vol vs low vol. Strategies that print in one regime lose in the other.
  • Risk of ruin — at your edge and sizing, what's the probability of losing X% before recovering? Don't trade until you can answer this.

6.5 Where this connects back to your bot

Concretely, three additions you'll likely want over the next 90 days:

  1. Daily participant OI ingest — fetch the CSV at 18:30 IST; store in data/fno/participant-oi/; expose a {-1, 0, +1} bias feature to signalEngine.js.
  2. IV regime gate — pull India VIX; refuse to open new long-premium trades when VIX > 75th percentile of last 90 days.
  3. Macro pre-open check — read GIFT NIFTY at 09:00 IST; skip the session if gap is > 1% (trend day, your mean-reversion edge breaks down).
