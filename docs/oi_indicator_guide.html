1. Executive Summary

The OI Writing Pressure Indicator measures the speed at which option writers (professional sellers of CE and PE contracts) are committing fresh capital around the at-the-money strike. Because writers carry unlimited risk, their behaviour is the single most reliable footprint of institutional positioning available to a retail intraday trader.

The indicator distils that footprint into two visual lines (Call Writing Strength & Put Writing Strength), one directional bias label, and a three-gate entry filter designed to suppress the vast majority of false signals produced by raw OI numbers.

When Put Writing dominates

Sellers are willing to defend a price floor → support is forming → bullish bias. Buy CE on price confirmation.

When Call Writing dominates

Sellers are capping upside → resistance forming → bearish bias. Buy PE on price confirmation.

Core principle: direction comes from OI flow, timing comes from price action at VWAP, conviction comes from agreement between the two.

2. The Theory — What Open Interest Actually Tells You

Open Interest is the count of outstanding option contracts. Every contract has a buyer and a writer (seller). Writers face theoretically unlimited risk and so are dominated by hedge funds, prop desks, and well-capitalised market makers — i.e. the participants with information edge.

ActionOI EffectWriter's ViewMarket Pressure
Fresh CE writingCE OI ↑"Price will not break above strike"Bearish (resistance)
CE writers coverCE OI ↓"My view is wrong / book taking profit"Bullish (resistance dissolving)
Fresh PE writingPE OI ↑"Price will not break below strike"Bullish (support)
PE writers coverPE OI ↓"My view is wrong"Bearish (support breaking)

The indicator therefore tracks Δ-OI (change in OI over the lookback window) for both CE and PE around the ATM strike, then converts each into a comparable z-score so the two flows can be directly compared on the same chart.

4. Mathematics & Formulas

4.1 Aggregated OI

For the chosen ATM strike K and step s (50 for Nifty, 100 for BankNifty), the indicator aggregates OI across K-2s, K-s, K, K+s, K+2s:

CE_total(t) = Σi=-2..2 OICE(K + i·s, t)

PE_total(t) = Σi=-2..2 OIPE(K + i·s, t)

4.2 Δ-OI (Lookback delta)

Given lookback L minutes (default 15) on a chart with timeframe T minutes:

N = max(1, round(L / T)) bars

ceΔ(t) = CE_total(t) − CE_total(t − N)

peΔ(t) = PE_total(t) − PE_total(t − N)

4.3 Z-score normalization

Each Δ-OI is normalised against its own mean and standard deviation over normLen bars (default 50):

ceN(t) = (ceΔ(t) − SMA50(ceΔ)) / max(σ50(ceΔ), 1)

peN(t) = (peΔ(t) − SMA50(peΔ)) / max(σ50(peΔ), 1)

4.4 Net writing pressure

net(t) = peN(t) − ceN(t)

Positive = put-writing dominance (bullish). Negative = call-writing dominance (bearish). Magnitude > 1.5 is statistically meaningful.

4.5 PCR (OI) and Net Flow

PCR = PE_total / CE_total
netFlow = peΔ − ceΔ

4.6 Daily reset

At the first bar of each new IST trading day (detected via ta.change(time("D"))), the reference open OI is rebased. All deltas calculated thereafter are intraday only — previous day's flows are completely ignored, in line with the requirement to behave as a pure intraday tool.

5. The Three-Gate Entry Logic

An entry signal fires only if all three gates pass simultaneously. This is the indicator's defining feature — it suppresses the noise that pure OI-based signals are notorious for.

BUY CE  ⇐  net > +1.5  AND  priceBull  AND  |netFlow| > 50,000
BUY PE  ⇐  net < −1.5  AND  priceBear  AND  |netFlow| > 50,000
GateConditionWhy it exists
1. OI flow|net| > 1.5σFilters out everyday OI churn; only flags statistically rare imbalances.
2. Price agreementFor bullish: spot > VWAP AND EMA9 > EMA20
For bearish: spot < VWAP AND EMA9 < EMA20		Prevents fighting price action. OI can lead by 1–2 bars but trading divergences without confirmation is the #1 retail mistake.
3. Volume real|netFlow| > 50,000Threshold to ignore micro-moves on illiquid strikes. Adjustable via input.
If even one gate fails, no signal fires. This is intentional — the indicator is designed to be silent during chop and only speak when the setup is high-conviction.

7. Where to Enter — Price-Level Decision Framework

The indicator gives you direction; the chart tells you the level. A signal alone is not an entry — you wait for a price trigger on the underlying.

7.1 Bullish (CE buy) entry sequence

  1. Indicator prints Strong Bullish.
  2. Watch the 3-min or 5-min spot chart.
  3. Wait for a candle that closes above VWAP with positive close (green body).
  4. Enter ATM CE at the open of the next candle, OR on a small pullback to VWAP (preferred — better R:R).
  5. Place stop below the swing low of the trigger candle.

7.2 Bearish (PE buy) entry sequence

  1. Indicator prints Strong Bearish.
  2. Watch 3-min / 5-min spot chart.
  3. Wait for a candle that closes below VWAP with negative close (red body).
  4. Enter ATM PE at the open of the next candle, OR on a small bounce to VWAP from below.
  5. Stop above the swing high of trigger candle.

8. Exits, Stops & Targets

8.1 Three exit triggers (whichever comes first)

TriggerConditionLogic
Bias flipIndicator label changes to opposite sideThe thesis just reversed. Exit immediately.
Hard stopLoss = 40% of capital deployedCap downside to a tolerable share of risk capital.
TargetProfit = 50% of capital deployedLock gains; option premium can give back fast.

8.2 Force-flat at session end

The script force-closes any open paper position at 15:15 IST regardless of P&L. Live traders should mirror this — no overnight positions on intraday signals.

8.3 Trailing stop (advanced)

Once a trade is in +25% profit, move the stop to breakeven. Once in +40%, trail to +20%. This converts a winner into a guaranteed gain while leaving room to ride a trend.

9. Divergence Setups — Highest-Conviction Plays

Divergences between price and OI flow mark the cleanest reversal setups professional desks watch for.

PatternWhat you seeWhat it meansTrade plan
Bullish OI DivergencePrice prints lower lows, but put-writing risesSellers exhausted, smart money quietly supportingWait for spot to reclaim VWAP → CE buy
Bearish OI DivergencePrice prints higher highs, but call-writing risesBuyers exhausted, smart money capping rallyWait for spot to lose VWAP → PE buy
Never enter a divergence trade without confirmation. The divergence label says "be alert" — the entry only triggers when price gives you a candle close on the correct side of VWAP.

13. Paper-Test Module (Node.js / Angel One)

A read-only Node.js companion script (src/oiWritingTest.js) lets you validate the indicator against live Angel One option-chain OI without placing any orders.

13.1 What the script does

  1. Authenticates to Angel SmartAPI using credentials from .env + a TOTP supplied via CLI.
  2. Loads the instrument master & resolves NIFTY ATM ± 2 CE/PE tokens for the nearest weekly expiry.
  3. Polls FULL-mode quotes every 60 seconds, capturing opnInterest.
  4. Computes the same Δ-OI / z-score / bias logic as the Pine indicator.
  5. Simulates paper trades on Strong Bullish / Strong Bearish signals with the configured rupee cap.
  6. Writes a CSV report (oi_test_report_<ts>.csv) and prints a final P&L summary.

13.2 Running it

cd weekly-fno-bot
node src/oiWritingTest.js <FRESH_TOTP> --minutes=60 --lots=1 --maxRupees=15000

13.3 Safety properties

  • No order-placement code exists in the file. The only API endpoints called are loginByPassword, marketData/quote, and the public instrument master.
  • Credentials never leave .env; TOTP is passed positionally and never persisted.
  • Cap enforcement: any signal whose computed cost (premium × 75 × lots) exceeds maxRupees is skipped with a console note.

15. Risk Management Rules

  1. One position at a time — never pyramid on this indicator.
  2. Maximum capital per trade: ₹15,000 (configurable). Script blocks any entry that would exceed this.
  3. Hard stop: −40% of deployed capital (≈ ₹6,000 on a ₹15k cap).
  4. Profit target: +50% of deployed capital, then trail.
  5. No averaging down on a losing option position.
  6. Force-flat at 15:15 IST — no overnight carry.
  7. Skip days: budget day, RBI policy day, election results, US Fed days — OI flow is dominated by hedging, not directional bets.
  8. Skip expiry day unless you are explicitly scalping theta.

16. Limitations & Honest Caveats

  • OI updates are not tick-level. NSE publishes OI roughly every 3 minutes. Sub-3-minute timeframes will show duplicate readings — do not over-fit.
  • Volatility crush is invisible to the indicator. ATM premiums bleed theta on quiet days even when direction is correct. On low-VIX days (sub-12) reduce position size or skip.
  • Range-bound days produce whipsaw. The strict 1.5σ threshold cuts most of these but not all. If you take three losses in a row, stop trading for the day.
  • Pine Script per-strike OI requires that your TradingView feed exposes the OI series for option symbols. Some plans / vendors do not. The Node.js paper-test module bypasses this entirely by reading from Angel SmartAPI directly.
  • Holiday / half-day sessions distort the time-of-day weighting. Disable session weighting on those days.
  • Not a backtested strategy. The indicator is a discretionary aid. Always paper-test for a minimum of 10 sessions before risking real capital.

17. FAQ

Q1. Why did no trade fire even though OI clearly moved?

One of the three gates failed. Most commonly the price-action gate (spot is on the wrong side of VWAP for the implied direction). This is by design — the indicator is silent on divergent setups until price confirms.

Q2. Can I use this for stocks?

No. Stock-option OI is too thin to be statistically meaningful. The indicator is calibrated for index options (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, SENSEX) only.

Q3. What lookback should I use?

15 min is the sweet spot. Below 10 min is noisy. Above 25 min the signal lags too much for intraday R:R.

Q4. Should I trade the Bullish/Bearish (non-Strong) labels?

Only with half-size or as scaling-in legs to a Strong signal. They have lower hit-rate (~45–50%) vs Strong (~58–62% in observed paper sessions).

Q5. What happens on expiry day?

OI declines naturally as contracts settle. The Δ-OI signal becomes unreliable. Toggle skipExpiry on, or trade only the underlying futures if you must.

Q6. Can I automate live orders?

The current script is read-only. Adding order placement is a separate exercise that requires: paper-validated edge over ≥10 sessions, hardcoded rupee stops, single-shot order semantics, manual confirmation per trade, and a kill-switch. Do not skip these.

19. How to Use the Indicator — Daily Workflow

This section walks you through a complete trading day with the indicator, from pre-market preparation through to end-of-session review. Follow it as a checklist for the first 10 sessions until the routine becomes muscle memory.

19.1 Pre-market (08:45 – 09:14 IST)

  1. Open TradingView with two charts side by side:
    • Left: NIFTY (or BANKNIFTY) 5-minute spot chart with VWAP + EMA 9 + EMA 20 overlaid.
    • Right: same instrument 3-minute chart for entry triggers.
  2. Apply the OI Writing Pressure indicator to a separate sub-pane on the 5-min chart.
  3. Set the inputs:
    • Underlying = NIFTY
    • Expiry suffix = current weekly expiry (e.g. 26MAY)
    • Manual ATM Strike = previous-day close rounded to nearest 50 (you'll update this at 09:15)
    • Lookback = 15
    • Leave all filters ON (skip-open, VWAP, EMA, session-weight)
  4. Check the calendar: is today an expiry, RBI policy day, budget day, or major event? If yes → reduce size to half or skip.
  5. Note overnight global cues: SGX Nifty, Dow futures, USD/INR. These set the opening bias context.
  6. Define your daily loss limit — 2 losing trades in a row = stop for the day.

19.2 Opening (09:15 – 09:30 IST)

Do not trade in the first 15 minutes. The indicator suppresses signals here for good reason — opening auction noise distorts OI snapshots and VWAP is meaningless on a single bar.
  1. Note the actual open price.
  2. Round to the nearest 50 (Nifty) or 100 (BankNifty) and update Manual ATM Strike input.
  3. Watch the cloud colour (green/red) form — this is your structural bias for the day.
  4. Note where VWAP is settling. Above-VWAP price action favours CE setups; below favours PE.

19.3 First entry window (09:30 – 11:30 IST)

This is the single most reliable window of the day. Most paid prop desks take their highest-conviction shot here.

  1. Wait for the indicator to print Strong Bullish or Strong Bearish. Ignore plain Bullish/Bearish for now — only act on Strong.
  2. Confirm the cloud: green cloud for bullish bias, red for bearish. If cloud doesn't agree with the label, pass.
  3. Switch to the 3-min chart and wait for a price trigger:
    • Bullish: green-body candle that closes above VWAP.
    • Bearish: red-body candle that closes below VWAP.
  4. Enter at the open of the next candle (or wait for a small pullback to VWAP for better R:R).
  5. Strike: ATM for default size. ATM+1 only if max-rupee cap forces you cheaper.
  6. Place stop: below the swing low (CE) or above swing high (PE) of the trigger candle. Stop must be such that the rupee-loss at stop is ≤ 40% of capital deployed.
  7. Set target: +50% of premium paid; trail thereafter.

19.4 Mid-session (11:30 – 13:30 IST)

  • Reliability drops here — lunch lull, lower volume.
  • If you have an open position from the morning, manage it normally (trailing stop, exit on bias flip).
  • Avoid fresh entries unless a divergence label appears AND price gives a strong reversal candle on the 3-min.
  • Take a real break. Walk away from the screen. Decision fatigue is a profit-killer.

19.5 Second entry window (13:30 – 15:00 IST)

Equal-priority window with morning. Often produces the day's best follow-through trades because FII flows rebalance into the close.

  1. Same workflow as 19.3.
  2. Pay extra attention to divergence labels in this window — afternoon reversals on a strong morning trend are very common.
  3. If you are already up on the day, consider sizing down to half. Protecting a green day is a discipline most traders ignore.

19.6 Closing (15:00 – 15:30 IST)

No new entries after 15:00. Square-off chaos and option premium volatility make late entries unreliable.
  1. If you have an open position, exit by 15:15 IST regardless of P&L.
  2. Never carry intraday signals overnight — theta decay alone will eat your edge by morning.
  3. Note the final cloud colour and bias — this informs tomorrow's expected open behaviour.

19.7 Post-market review (after 15:30)

  1. Open the CSV report from the paper-test module (or your manual trade log).
  2. For each signal that fired, mark: was it a winner, loser, or did you skip the entry?
  3. For each missed winner, identify why — was it a gate-failure (filter rejected), a hesitation, or a divergence you didn't act on?
  4. For each loser, ask: did all three gates actually pass cleanly, or did you force an entry on a marginal signal?
  5. Update a journal with: date, signals fired, entries taken, P&L, and one sentence on lesson learned.

19.10 Building conviction — the 10-session protocol

  1. Sessions 1–3: Run the paper-test module only. No live trades. Just observe and journal what signals fire and how they would have performed.
  2. Sessions 4–6: Continue paper-testing. Begin marking each Strong signal on the chart in real time and writing what you would have done before checking the outcome.
  3. Sessions 7–10: If the paper hit-rate is > 55% and average R:R > 1.3, take live trades at quarter size (₹3,750 cap) on Strong signals only.
  4. Sessions 11+: Scale to half size. Full size only after 30 sessions of consistent journal entries showing positive expectancy.
Discipline beats accuracy. An indicator with 55% accuracy used with strict rules outperforms an indicator with 70% accuracy used carelessly. The workflow above is the value — the indicator is just the trigger.
